Lanyard design decisions

A hiring tool for in-house recruiters, made like a new hire’s ID badge: the slot on each card shows who acts next.

The owner asked for a fresh, slick look instead of the sage-and-black reference. We chose warm paper, one magenta accent and round, lit cards over the usual dark violet console, because recruiters work in it all day and must see whose turn it is.

Brief: An in-house recruiter, between two calls, clearing tasks and explaining decisions

  • Character: The front desk on a new hire’s first day: the badge is printed, the desk is ready, and every step is accounted for.
  • For: In-house recruiters at a growing company (level manager or admin), who build the hiring flow and work it. Interviewers (level employee) who only get tasks.
  • Job: Clear the task inbox, keep candidates moving, and explain any decision (“why was this person rejected?”).
  • Moment: At a laptop between calls and interviews, with many tabs open. Interviewers fill a scorecard right after the interview, while it is fresh.
  • Feel: First second: bright, crisp, calm. Using it says: this company hires with care and can show why.
The rest of the brief
  • Who sets the look: Open. The owner asked to reimagine a frontend built from the theme-reference images, but the repo had no frontend (only the Nuxt welcome page), so there was no existing UI to audit. The owner chose to build it fresh here, and the reference look is set aside on purpose.
  • Scope: product: every screen in docs/backend.md: sign-in, inbox and scorecards, jobs, job hub (pipeline, flow canvas, form, details), candidate page, hires report, team, settings, public apply page.
  • Screens: Recruiter app 960 px to 2560 px, laptop and desktop, no phone; the shell sets a 960 px minimum width. Apply page 360 px and up, because candidates apply on phones. Source: assumed.
  • Genre: Applicant tracking system plus a workflow builder. Kept conventions: a left navigation, data tables, a list beside its detail, tabs on the job, a node canvas with handles.
  • Richness: Work surfaces (inbox, tables, forms) stay calm and fairly dense, because recruiters work in them for hours. The candidate page and the canvas carry more (score bars, decision traces, step cards), because people show them to hiring managers.
  • Identity: Provisional, for the owner: the name “Lanyard”, a dark badge mark with a magenta slot, magenta oklch(.55 .21 352), Instrument Sans with JetBrains Mono.
  • Core copy: “Submit scorecard”, “Move to…”, “Close as rejected”, “Publish v3”, “Send application”. Likeliest error: “Stopped: the CV can’t be read. It may be a scan. Move it to a manual review.”
  • First value: A new job starts with the Standard hire flow already wired (CV read, AI review, shortlist, interview, offer), so the first thing a recruiter does is see a complete flow on the canvas and publish it.
  • Signature moment: Submitting a scorecard: the next task opens by itself. Second: the “Why” card on the candidate page, which turns the decision trace into plain words.
  • Ending: Scorecard sent: the next task opens (quiet). Job published: “v4 is live” with the apply link and Copy. Candidate hired: the header becomes an ID badge (the one Moment). Application sent: a real confirmation page.
  • Flow shape: Scorecard: one page beside the list, not steps, because interviewers know the questions and fill them in one go. Apply: one page, because a CV form has few decisions. New job: one small dialog, then the hub. Rejected: a multi-step job wizard, because the Standard hire flow removes most of the decisions.
  • Map: Job has a form, a flow (draft and published versions) and many applications. Application has one candidate, many steps, events and files. Task is a person step on an application. User has a role. Navigation: flat sections in a labelled rail (Inbox, Jobs, Hires, Team, Settings); networked links between task, candidate and job. Rejected: a top bar, because the canvas and tables need the full height and the rail keeps labels. Hand-offs: task → candidate; candidate → job pipeline; new job → details; publish → apply link; stopped step → retry in place.
  • Fixed: Nuxt 4 and Vue 3, the backend API, Vue Flow for the canvas (the owner asked for it).
  • Constraints: Sign-in is Google only, so reviews use a faked API. No new dependencies other than Vue Flow.

Direction: Badge day, not the dark console, because the badge slot can show who acts

We tried 4 directions and 1 rolled candidate on the same task. Pick one to see it.

The directions

The rejected directions are reconstructions, not renders. “Typical” is our estimate of how often a model gives that direction for this brief.

The badge ties the product to its result: a person who starts work. The slot on each card says who acts, so a recruiter can read a flow or an inbox at a glance. It costs some familiarity: there is no dark mode and no violet.

  • The slot punched in an ID badge →A short pill at the top of a cardIts colour says who acts: magenta for a person, ink for the system, green for hired, red for stopped. On step cards, task headers and report cards.
  • The same slot →The current tab and nav itemThe current place carries the slot, so “where am I” uses the same mark as “who acts”.
  • The badge on the first day →The hired candidate’s headerWhen a recruiter closes a candidate as hired, the header becomes a badge. It is the one Moment in the product.
How it was chosen: obvious ideas, the default to beat, the product’s world, samples, the roll
  • Obvious ideas set aside: Zinc neutrals with an indigo accent and white cards; the dark Linear-style console with violet and Inter; the pastel sage dashboard with black pills from the theme-reference images; and the predictable opposite, cream paper with a serif.
  • Default to beat: Background #f8fafc, text #0f172a, accent indigo #4f46e5, Inter, 8 px radius, left sidebar with icons and labels, uppercase grey table labels. From the direction ledger: sage-tinted paper, a near-black primary and Atkinson Hyperlegible (an earlier hiring tool for the same owner).
  • The product’s world: The CV, the calendar invite, the interview room booking, the scorecard, the shortlist, the debrief, the offer letter, the reference call, the notice period in weeks, the closing date, the ID badge and its lanyard, the desk set up for the first day, the welcome coffee.
  • Directions sampled: Badge day · airy, near stark, warm saturated accent, layered, bare, round, neutral type · the ID badge and its slot · 8%. Midnight console · packed, stark, cool violet, flat, bare, medium radius, neutral type · 45%, dropped as the most typical. Offer letter · airy, soft, warm muted, flat, decorated, sharp, serif · the letter · 25%. Debrief room · medium, soft, multi-hue markers, flat, decorated, round · the whiteboard · 20%.
  • Rolled candidate: Rolled axes: density near airy, contrast near stark, colour far warm and saturated, depth far layered, ornament far bare, geometry far round, type far neutral. Rolled domain 8, print and editorial: sidenotes beside the text became the decision trace beside its step. The roll gave the values of Badge day; the badge slot came from the product’s world, the mutation that combined the two.
  • Chosen: Badge day. It makes “who acts next” readable at a glance, which is the core of an inbox and a flow. It fits a calm, careful company. It costs the familiarity of a dark console.
  • Motif: The badge slot: a 30 × 5 px pill at the top centre of a card, coloured by who acts.
  • Placements: Step cards on the canvas (who acts in each step); the task header and the current tab (where you are and whose turn it is); the hired candidate’s badge (the ending).
  • Style tile: The tile showed the slot reads at 30 × 5 px and the magenta stays on actions. The warm paper read slightly pink; we kept it, because it sets the tool apart from grey dashboards and the plum greys match it.
  • Default check (after layer 4): Against the default to beat: background changed (cool plum paper, not slate); accent changed (magenta, not indigo); type changed (Instrument Sans, not Inter); radius logic changed (pills and 20 px cards, not 8 px); labels changed (sentence-case table headers). Still equal: a left navigation, kept because the canvas and tables need the full height (d-2).

Design language: Warm paper, plum greys, one magenta accent, round pills

Magenta means “act here”. Everything else is ink on warm paper, so the one action per view is easy to find.

Session 2026-10-20: A new frontend in 5 chunks on one system, then two review rounds

Reimagine, product scope · the owner asked for a fresh, slick, modern frontend

The repo had a finished backend and no frontend. The coordinator built the shared system and checked it on a kitchen-sink page; five agents then built the screens on it. Review found that magenta and the slot meant too many things, and that the pipeline and canvas read generic. We gave each colour one job, put stopped candidates first, and redrew the hired header as a real ID badge.

Life three places, one of them animated

  1. The next task opens by itselfAfter a scorecard, the next task replaces it, and a line says which. It tells the person the product kept their place. Every task. Reduced motion: the same, with no movement.
  2. The hire badgeWhen a recruiter closes a candidate as hired, the header becomes a badge with a short settle. It tells them the hire is done. Rare. Reduced motion: a fade.
  3. The inbox count on the railThe count on Inbox changes when a task arrives or leaves. It tells the person there is work before they open it. Often. Still.

For you to decide: 5 choices, and 1 things owed

You can reverse each one. Each card shows what it gains, what it costs, and the alternative if you do not agree.

  1. The name “Lanyard” and the badge markIt gives the tool a face and ties it to the result, a person who starts work. It is a guess at your brand. d-10Your company name and logo, if the tool is internal only.
  2. Magenta as the one accentIt is far from the status colours and reads fresh. It is louder than a neutral tool. d-13A deep blue-black accent, if magenta clashes with your brand.
  3. No server renderingIt keeps the app simple and made the faked-API reviews possible. Shared apply links show no job title in chat previews. d-1Server rendering for /apply only, if candidates share links in chat apps.
  4. Dots on the canvasThey are the 24 px snap grid. The reference images also had dots, which you asked to leave behind. d-43A plain canvas without snapping, if the dots read as the old look.
  5. Green for live jobs as well as hiresGreen is one family: a good or finished state. Hired and live share it. d-38Ink for live, if you want green to mean hired only.
