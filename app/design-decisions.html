Brief: A small-business owner who hires part time, moving applicants with almost no setup

  • Character: The front desk on a new hire’s first day: the badge is printed, the desk is ready, and every step is accounted for.
  • For: The person who hires at a small or medium business: a founder, an office manager or an HR generalist, who is often also the hiring manager (level manager or admin). Interviewers (level employee) who only get tasks. Changed 2026-10-24; before: in-house recruiters at a growing company.
  • Job: Get applicants, move them along and explain any decision (“why was this person rejected?”), with almost no setup.
  • Moment: At a laptop between other work, hiring part time. They know boards from Workable, Breezy or Recruitee. Interviewers fill a scorecard right after the interview, while it is fresh.
  • Feel: First second: bright, crisp, calm. Using it says: this company hires with care and can show why.
The rest of the brief
  • Who sets the look: Existing, level Revamp, since 2026-10-24: the Lanyard look stays, and flow, structure and layout change where the approved mocks change them. On 2026-10-20 the look was Open. The owner asked for a new frontend instead of one built from the theme-reference images, but the repo had no frontend (only the Nuxt welcome page), so there was no existing UI to audit. The owner chose to build it fresh here, and the reference look is set aside on purpose.
  • Scope: flow, since 2026-10-24: the small-business release across the job hub, the candidate page, Settings, the Library, import, Hires and four public pages. On 2026-10-20: product, every screen in docs/backend.md: sign-in, inbox and scorecards, jobs, job hub (pipeline, flow canvas, form, details), candidate page, hires report, team, settings, public apply page.
  • Screens: Recruiter app 960 px to 2560 px, laptop and desktop, no phone; the shell sets a 960 px minimum width. Public pages (apply, careers, booking, offer) 360 px and up, because candidates use phones. Source: assumed on 2026-10-20, set by the coordinator on 2026-10-24.
  • Genre: Applicant tracking system plus a workflow builder. Kept conventions: a left navigation, data tables, a list beside its detail, tabs on the job, a node canvas with handles.
  • Richness: Work surfaces (inbox, tables, forms) stay calm and fairly dense, because recruiters work in them for hours. The candidate page and the canvas carry more (score bars, decision traces, step cards), because people show them to hiring managers.
  • Identity: Provisional, for the owner: the name “Lanyard”, a dark badge mark with a magenta slot, magenta oklch(.55 .21 352), Instrument Sans with JetBrains Mono.
  • Core copy: “Submit scorecard”, “Moving Ben Okafor from AI review”, “Follows the flow, so no reason needed.”, “Reject and email Amara”, “Accept offer”, “Move to…”, “Close as rejected”, “Publish v3”, “Send application”. Likeliest error: “Stopped: the CV can’t be read. It may be a scan. Move it to a manual review.”
  • First value: A new job starts from a template that is already wired. Since 2026-10-24 the default is Simple, with no AI notice to set up, and the job opens on its Stages tab, so the first thing the owner sees is their stages in words.
  • Signature moment: Submitting a scorecard: the next task opens by itself. Since 2026-10-24 also the drag on the board: each column says where it leads, the preview says what the drop will do, and the card lands on the accent tint. Second: the “Why” card on the candidate page, which turns the decision trace into plain words.
  • Ending: Scorecard sent: the next task opens (quiet). Card dropped: it settles in its new row. Offer accepted: “Welcome to ‹company›” and the signed letter. Interview booked: “You’re booked”, with Add to calendar. Job published: “v4 is live” with the apply link and Copy. Candidate hired: the header becomes an ID badge (the one Moment). Application sent: a real confirmation page.
  • Delight piece: None as a separate part. Since 2026-10-24 the boot screen carries a small check-in (proposed, waits for the owner): the badge in the rail is scanned while the app checks who you are. It is life, not a delight piece, because it shows on every visit and must stay quiet.
  • Flow shape: Scorecard: one page beside the list, not steps, because interviewers know the questions and fill them in one go. Import: three steps (upload, match, import), because each match changes who comes in. Decline an offer: one inline second step, not a modal, because a phone modal can trap the candidate. Apply: one page, because a CV form has few decisions. New job: since 2026-10-25, five steps (the role, how you hire, about the job, must-haves, check), then the hub. Each step names the tab where it lives later. Until then it was one small dialog, because the Standard hire flow removes most of the decisions; the QA run of 2026-10-25 showed that people then skip Details, and the AI scores against empty skills. First run for an admin: four steps (company, team, your hours, first job), because the order matters and nothing showed it.
  • Map: Job has a form, a flow (draft and published versions) and many applications. A flow has stages; a stage has steps and can ask questions on a move by hand. An application has a source, a consent, bookings and offers. Application has one candidate, many steps, events and files. Task is a person step on an application. User has a role. Navigation: flat sections in a labelled rail (Inbox, Jobs, Hires, Team, Settings); networked links between task, candidate and job. Rejected: a top bar, because the canvas and tables need the full height and the rail keeps labels. Hand-offs: task → candidate; candidate → job pipeline; new job → details; publish → apply link; stopped step → retry in place.
  • Fixed: Nuxt 4 and Vue 3, the backend API, Vue Flow for the canvas (the owner asked for it).
  • Constraints: Sign-in is Google only, so reviews use a faked API. No new dependencies other than Vue Flow.

The directions

The rejected directions are reconstructions, not renders. “Typical” is our estimate of how often a model gives that direction for this brief.

The badge ties the product to its result: a person who starts work. The slot on each card says who acts, so a recruiter can read a flow or an inbox at a glance. It costs some familiarity: there is no dark mode and no violet.

  • The slot punched in an ID badge →A short pill at the top of a cardIts colour says who acts: magenta for a person, ink for the system, green for hired, red for stopped. On step cards, task headers and report cards.
  • The same slot →The current tab and nav itemThe current place carries the slot, so “where am I” uses the same mark as “who acts”.
  • The badge on the first day →The hired candidate’s headerWhen a recruiter closes a candidate as hired, the header becomes a badge. It is the one Moment in the product.
How it was chosen: obvious ideas, the default to beat, the product’s world, samples, the roll
  • Obvious ideas set aside: Zinc neutrals with an indigo accent and white cards; the dark Linear-style console with violet and Inter; the pastel sage dashboard with black pills from the theme-reference images; and the predictable opposite, cream paper with a serif.
  • Default to beat: Background #f8fafc, text #0f172a, accent indigo #4f46e5, Inter, 8 px radius, left sidebar with icons and labels, uppercase grey table labels. From the direction ledger: sage-tinted paper, a near-black primary and Atkinson Hyperlegible (an earlier hiring tool for the same owner).
  • The product’s world: The CV, the calendar invite, the interview room booking, the scorecard, the shortlist, the debrief, the offer letter, the reference call, the notice period in weeks, the closing date, the ID badge and its lanyard, the desk set up for the first day, the welcome coffee.
  • Directions sampled: Badge day · airy, near stark, warm saturated accent, layered, bare, round, neutral type · the ID badge and its slot · 8%. Midnight console · packed, stark, cool violet, flat, bare, medium radius, neutral type · 45%, dropped as the most typical. Offer letter · airy, soft, warm muted, flat, decorated, sharp, serif · the letter · 25%. Debrief room · medium, soft, multi-hue markers, flat, decorated, round · the whiteboard · 20%.
  • Rolled candidate: Rolled axes: density near airy, contrast near stark, colour far warm and saturated, depth far layered, ornament far bare, geometry far round, type far neutral. Rolled domain 8, print and editorial: sidenotes beside the text became the decision trace beside its step. The roll gave the values of Badge day; the badge slot came from the product’s world, the mutation that combined the two.
  • Chosen: Badge day. It makes “who acts next” readable at a glance, which is the core of an inbox and a flow. It fits a calm, careful company. It costs the familiarity of a dark console.
  • Motif: The badge slot: a 30 × 5 px pill at the top centre of a card, coloured by who acts.
  • Placements: Step cards on the canvas (who acts in each step); the task header and the current tab (where you are and whose turn it is); the hired candidate’s badge (the ending).
  • Style tile: The tile showed the slot reads at 30 × 5 px and the magenta stays on actions. The warm paper read slightly pink; we kept it, because it sets the tool apart from grey dashboards and the plum greys match it.
  • Default check (after layer 4): Against the default to beat: background changed (cool plum paper, not slate); accent changed (magenta, not indigo); type changed (Instrument Sans, not Inter); radius logic changed (pills and 20 px cards, not 8 px); labels changed (sentence-case table headers). Still equal: a left navigation, kept because the canvas and tables need the full height (d-2).

Measured inventory (check.py --inventory app, 2026-10-24)

12 literal colours, 21 radii, 19 font sizes, 43 shadows. We read each outlier and kept them, for these reasons:

  • Radius 4–9 px: each small element has a radius of about half its height (bars, pips, the tab mark) or in proportion to its size (a 28 px mark gets 8 px, a 32 px mark gets 9 px). The token radii are for controls and panels, not for these marks.
  • Georgia, serif: one use. It draws the italic “I” icon in the Markdown toolbar (session 2026-10-21), where a serif italic says “italic” more clearly than the sans.
  • 12, 13, 15, 10.5 px font sizes: code labels and toolbar glyphs from earlier sessions. These are not new in this release. Making them tokens is in the owed list.
  • oklch(0.985 0.003 330): the table row hover in main.css (earlier session). It is lighter than --hover on purpose, so that a long table does not flash while you scan it.

Life three places, one of them animated

  1. The next task opens by itselfAfter a scorecard, the next task replaces it, and a line says which. It tells the person the product kept their place. Every task. Reduced motion: the same, with no movement.
  2. The hire badgeWhen a recruiter closes a candidate as hired, the header becomes a badge with a short settle. It tells them the hire is done. Rare. Reduced motion: a fade.
  3. The inbox count on the railThe count on Inbox changes when a task arrives or leaves. It tells the person there is work before they open it. Often. Still.
  4. The named-people swapSaving a flow that names people offers to swap them for each job’s owner, ticked already. It tells the recruiter the product noticed what does not travel. Rare. Still.

For you to decide: 5 choices, and 14 things owed

You can reverse each one. Each card shows what it gains, what it costs, and the alternative if you do not agree.

  1. Two ways to make an offerThe templates still end in an “Offer email” step, and the release adds “Make offer…” with a real offer page. Both work today. A small business can send two different offers to one person. d-198Replace the “Offer email” step in the templates with a task to make the offer, if every offer should use the offer page.
  2. Two templates look the sameIn the new-job dialog, “Panel interview” and “AI screening” show the same stage chips (Screening · Interview · Decision). The cards are honest, because the stages are the same, but the owner cannot see the difference. d-185Name the panel step in its stage (“Phone screen · Panel”), if the template should read different at a glance.
  3. Pay has no periodThe job’s salary has no period field, so “£12.60 – £13.50” does not say per hour or per year. Adding one is a schema change and a migration. d-250Add a period (hour, day, year) to the job and print it after the range, if you hire hourly staff.
  4. Bulk moves across flow versionsA bulk move can include people on an older flow version. If that version’s stage asks a required question, the server refuses those people, and the dialog says how many moved and why the rest did not. d-171Ask the older version’s questions in the same dialog, if mixed versions are common for you.
  5. The tab is “Stages”, not “Flow”Small businesses think in stages, as on Workable or Breezy. The Library and the canvas still say “flow”, so one thing has two words. d-263Keep “Flow” for the tab, if your team already says flow.

Assumed, please correct: 6 guesses, the riskiest is that 30 cards per step is enough

We assumed these because nobody could answer. Correct a line, and the design will change to agree.

  • 30 cards per step A small business has 30 people or fewer at most steps, so the board shows cards and only bigger steps become a pile.
  • Simple first A new owner wants the Simple template, with no AI notice, more than the power of AI screening.
  • Laptops The people who hire and interview use a laptop or desktop, 960 px and wider. Interviewers do not fill scorecards on a phone.
  • Phones for candidates Candidates apply, book and answer offers on phones, so the public pages work from 360 px.
  • Light only The product has one light scheme and no dark mode.
  • English The product is in English, with dates in the browser’s locale.
