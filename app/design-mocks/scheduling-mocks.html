Scheduling: you offer the times, the candidate picks one

Mock for approval. No code has changed yet. Halfmoon Coffee, Tuesday 27 October. Maya Reyes (Shift lead) interviews. Jonah Ofori sits on one panel with her. In tab 1, drag a person from Needs times onto the week.

One week, many candidates

  • Needs times is a new group at the top of Schedule. Drag a person onto the week. While you drag, a line shows the exact start in 15-minute steps, and turns grey on a clash. Drop to place a time. Drag again for each extra option. Each placed time shows a move and a trash button on hover. Move picks it up: the line follows the pointer, a click puts it down, Esc cancels.
  • Sharing: drop a person on someone else’s time to offer it to both. Both faces show on it. The first to pick gets it, and the other then sees what is left. If someone runs out of times, they come back to Needs times on their own.
  • Faster: the last person you dropped stays selected (magenta bar). A plain click on the week adds another time for them. This is also the way to do it without a mouse.
  • Send sends everyone’s times at once. The people move to Waiting, and their times stay on the week as outlines.
  • The date button opens the browser’s date picker, to jump to any week.
  • Times you already sent to someone else show as outlines. Click one to offer it again.
  • Panel: Ana’s interview has two people, so her times go to Jonah first. She gets only the times you both can make.
  • Interview hours go away. You choose each time yourself, with your own calendar beside you.

    Your week

    Panel: everyone says yes before the candidate hears anything

    • When a task has more than one person, the person who offers the times is the one who checks with the others. That person is Maya here. The others get a short task: Can you make these?
    • Each time gets a Yes or No. If a time clashes with an interview Jonah already has in Lanyard, the row says so. He still checks his own calendar for everything else.
    • Once everyone has answered, the candidate gets an email with only the times all of you can make, and all of your names. If no time is left, Maya gets the task back with Jonah’s note.
    • People usually agree on times in person or in chat first. This step makes that agreement visible, so nobody gets an invite they didn’t agree to.

    The candidate: pick one, ask for others, or step back

    • The email names everyone who will be there and lists the times. It goes out only when every person on the panel has agreed.
    • On the page, the candidate picks one time and books it. None of these work opens a note. The note goes to the interviewer, who sends new times.
    • I’m no longer interested is quiet and asks once more, inline. The team gets a notice. Nothing closes on its own.
    • After booking, Need a different time? uses the same note. The booked time stays until the candidate picks a new one, so nobody is left with nothing.
