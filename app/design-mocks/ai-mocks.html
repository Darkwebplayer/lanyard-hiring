Five frames at desktop size (1440 × 860). Example data: Northwind Café is hiring a weekend barista. Nothing here is built yet. Say which frames to change.

0 · Ask is closed until you open it. Two places for the button

  • Closed by default. It opens from the button, from ⌘J, or from a section action such as Why?. Close it and the page takes the full width again. It stays closed or open as you move between pages, until you close it.
  • A · In the left rail, above your avatar. The rail is on every page, so the button never moves. But it is far from where the panel opens.
  • B · In the top-right corner of the window, the same on every page and right where the panel opens. When the panel is open, its close button sits in the same place. It sits above each page's own actions, so “Share job” and “Publish” keep their place.

1 · Pressing “Why?” on a score opens Ask, already answering

  • Why? on each score row shows on hover and keyboard focus. Each section gets its own verb (Why?, Explain, Ask about this stage), not one generic “Ask AI” button.
  • The panel sits beside the page, not over it, so the score stays visible while you read why. The row you asked about keeps an ink outline.
  • “Looking at” chip: you always know what the AI can see. AI is drawn in ink, the colour Lanyard already uses for “the system acts”. Magenta stays for people.
  • Source chips scroll to and outline that part of the page. “How sure” comes from Clef’s probability, not from the AI’s own opinion.
  • Ask in the rail (⌘J) opens it on any page. The icon is a speech bubble with a question mark, not the usual AI sparkle.

3 · What the panel does when things are not normal

  • Writing: the answer streams in. The send button turns into Stop. With reduced motion, the cursor does not blink.
  • Failed: the thread stays, and your question stays in the box. One button tries again.
  • AI is off: people who are not admins never see Ask: no rail button, no Why? buttons. Admins see this one panel, which points to Settings.
  • Interviewer: an interviewer can ask only about candidates in their tasks. The chip says so. Blind steps and protected answers never reach the AI; the server removes them before the AI sees anything.

4 · A new job’s empty flow asks how you hire, in your own words

  • This replaces the blank canvas for a new job and for “New flow” in the Library. Presets and an empty start stay one click away.

  • New step in the list: “Check answers” (Clef). It asks yes/no or pick-one questions about answers or a CV, so a Decision step can branch on the result.

5 · The draft opens unsaved, with anything to fix marked on its step

  • The dark bar says it is a draft. Nothing goes live until you press Save. Discard goes back to frame 4 with your words still in the box.
  • Our own checker reviews every draft (the same one that runs before Publish). Anything it cannot fix by itself is marked on its step in amber, here a trial shift with no one assigned, and listed in the bar.
  • Your description stays in the corner, so you can edit it and draft again, or compare the steps with what you wrote.

6 · Settings for the two changed steps, in the flow editor’s side panel

  • Read CV, “Also pull out”: the three facts it always reads are shown as fixed chips. You add up to 10 more, each with a type. A fact the CV does not state stays empty, never guessed.
  • Check answers: the same card pattern as AI review criteria (Name, Key), so it reads as one family. Each question is “Yes or no” or “Pick one”.
  • “A Decision after this can use” lists what the step gives the next step, in the words the Decision will show. You see the result of your settings before you build the branch.
